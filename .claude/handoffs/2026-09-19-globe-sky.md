## The work, in order of what the eye gets
1. **Real stars.** The Yale Bright Star Catalogue (9,110 stars to magnitude 6.5; public domain; fetch the
   ASCII from a canonical mirror, keep the URL in the report) or Hipparcos to magnitude 7. Render as a point cloud
   (THREE.Points, additive, size from magnitude, colour from B-V through a small lookup: blue-white to orange),
   placed by right ascension / declination on a sphere of radius ~40 around the camera, rotated for the moment
   being shown: Greenwich sidereal time from the frame's UTC, so the sky turns with the day exactly as the Earth
   does (in Sun-fixed mode the stars drift instead). The Milky Way as a faint band: either a lightweight all-sky
   image (ESO's Milky Way panorama is CC BY 4.0; downsample to 2048x1024, credit it in the copy) mapped on the
   same sphere at low opacity, or leave it out and say so. Stars must be subtle: pinpricks, no bloom, dimmed
   where the Earth's limb glow is. Check the geometry with one fact: at 2026-09-12T0400Z the Sun is at RA ~11.3h,
   so Orion should be roughly opposite it; put a named-star check in `__dbg`.
2. **The Sun as a light source you can see.** A lit disc (angular diameter 0.53 deg; do not exaggerate) with a
   soft corona and a faint lens glare that appears when the disc is in the frame, clipped by the Earth. On the
   limb, a sunrise flare where the terminator meets the edge of the disc.
3. **An atmosphere that scatters.** Replace the fresnel rim with a single-scattering model on the back-face
   shell (a handful of samples per fragment along the view ray through a thin shell; Rayleigh only, Mie as a
   small forward term; precompute what you can). Blue by day, a warm band along the terminator that fades to red
   at the very edge, nothing on the night side except a faint airglow line. Also apply it to the ground: aerial
   perspective toward the limb (the ground gets bluer and paler near the edge of the disc) and a warm tint on
   the ground and cloud within ~10 deg of the terminator. Keep one draw call for the planet and one for the shell.
4. **The Moon.** Position from a low-precision lunar ephemeris (Meeus, truncated: ~1 deg is plenty), correct
   phase for the date (12 Sep 2026 is two days past new; check against a published phase), a small textured disc
   (NASA CGI Moon Kit colour map, 2048 wide, public domain) lit by the same sun vector, and moonlight on the
   night side: a faint cool specular on the sea and a slight lift of the night ground and cloud that scales with
   phase. Say in the copy that the Moon is real and its light is computed.
5. **Water and ground at night.** The sea's glint is a single power term. Add a cheap ocean normal perturbation
   (a tiled noise texture, animated slowly with UTC) so the glint breaks up, and use the Blue Marble bathymetry
   already in the basemap to keep shallow water lighter. Night ground: a very faint blue from the sky, not black.
6. **Cloud light, only the two terms that are yours:** a silver lining (forward-scatter brightening on cloud
   within ~15 deg of the terminator, using the opacity gradient toward the sun) and a base-darkening for thick
   cloud (opacity as thickness). Do not change how opacity is read or warped.

## Report (2026-09-19, session globe-sky)
1. **Shipped**, all six items, in `site/globe/index.html` (one commit; the sky is one block after `tiltG`, the air is `AIR_GLSL`
   shared by planet and shell, `skyTick()` is the only per-frame call). https://earthai-scales.vercel.app/globe/
2. **Stars**: Yale BSC5 (9,096 of 9,110 have a J2000 position and a V; the rest are novae), packed by `scripts/sky_assets.py` into
   `site/globe/sky/stars.bin` (int16 x5, **91 KB**, fetched at once; phones keep the brightest 3,000). Points sized and weighted
   by magnitude, coloured by B-V. Placed on a radius-40 shell that rides with the camera and turns by GMST. Geometry checks
   in `__dbg.sky` and `__dbg.star('Betelgeuse')` (RA/Dec, sub-point, screen position): at 2026-09-12T0400Z the page's Sun is at
   RA 11.36 h (brief: ~11.3 h). The brief's "Orion opposite" is not quite right — Orion (RA 5.5 h) is 90 deg from the Sun in
   September; the anti-Sun is Pegasus. Milky Way: ESO eso0932a (CC BY 4.0, Brunier), its own photographed stars median-filtered
   out so they do not double the points, 2048x1024, **40 KB**, rotated with the IAU J2000 galactic pole/centre matrix; verified
   through the page: Deneb reads l=84.3 b=2.0, Alpha Cen 315.7/-0.7, Sirius 227.2/-8.9 (all exact), and on the plate Alpha Cen
   sits in the band beside the Coalsack (`docs/globe-2026-09-19-sky/g_milkyway_alpha_cen_x2.jpg`, brightened 2x).
3. **Sun**: a disc at 0.53 deg on a camera-facing quad at the shell radius, depth-tested so the limb cuts it, with a small
   corona; a faint full-frame veil (`skyGlare`, one extra pass only while the disc is in frame and not behind the Earth).
4. **Air**: O'Neil single scattering (GPU Gems 2) on the back-face shell at 1.025, 6 samples (4 on a phone), Rayleigh + Mie
   (g=0.9) with the fitted optical-depth function instead of an inner loop; premultiplied output so the stars fade under the
   glow; a faint airglow line ~90 km up on the night limb. On the planet: `groundAir()` (3 samples, 2 on a phone) gives the
   transmission (normalised to noon, so the disc centre is untouched; reddens the ground and 70% of the cloud at the
   terminator) and the in-scatter, weighted toward the limb because the model's shell is 20x too thick (a first pass hazed
   the whole disc; before/after in `d_terminator_before_after.jpg`).
5. **Moon**: Astronomical Almanac low-precision series; new moon comes out 2026-09-11 03:00 UTC (published 03:27), full
   2026-09-26 16:00 (published 16:49). Textured sphere (NASA CGI Moon Kit, **99 KB at 1024x512** — the disc is never over ~30
   device px, so 2048 would be waste), near side to Earth, pole to the celestial pole, lit by the same sun, earthshine on the
   dark side. On 15 Sep it is a 23% crescent beside Antares (`f_moon_crescent_antares.jpg`). Moonlight on the night side:
   ground lift, cloud lift and a cool sea glint, all times phase^1.6 (0.005 on 12 Sep, 0.095 on 15 Sep, 1 at full).
6. **Sea and cloud**: a 256x256 sum-of-sinusoids wave texture (generated in JS, not fetched; labelled as not measured)
