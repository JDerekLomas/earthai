# The 12 August 2026 total solar eclipse, seen from GOES-East

## Posture (read first)
Derek asked "can we capture the eclipse still?" He wants to SEE the moon's shadow cross the Earth
in the satellite record: a short clip on a page, the darkening sweeping over the North Atlantic,
Greenland and Iceland, with the clouds underneath unchanged. He is a designer; the clip has to be
beautiful and the page must say in plain sentences what is shown, from which satellite, and what
the limits are. He will watch it for thirty seconds.

## The situation
- NASA GIBS has ALREADY DROPPED 12 August (checked 15 Sep: every GOES-East tile for 2026-08-12
  returns 404; 13 August returns 200). GIBS is not an option.
- Our own archives: `data/goes/conus/` (GOES-East, z5, 7x4 tiles, lon -135..-56, lat 22..56)
  has 54 frames on 12 Aug; check whether 16:00-19:00 UTC are among them and whether the north-east
  corner (Newfoundland / Labrador) darkens. The umbra itself was over Greenland ~16:30-17:30 UTC,
  Iceland ~17:45, Spain ~18:30; the penumbra covered the whole North Atlantic and NE North America.
- The permanent archive: NOAA GOES on AWS Open Data, bucket `noaa-goes19` (GOES-East since 2025;
  verify the bucket name and that 2026-08-12 exists: `aws s3 ls --no-sign-request s3://noaa-goes19/ABI-L2-CMIPF/2026/224/17/`
  -- day-of-year 224). Products: `ABI-L2-CMIPF` (Cloud and Moisture Imagery, Full Disk, one file per
  band per 10 min). Band 02 (red visible, 0.5 km) files are ~90 MB; **band 03 (0.86 um, 1 km) or
  band 01 (blue, 1 km) are ~40 MB and enough**. Also fetch band 13 for the same instants as the
  control that the clouds did not change, only the light. `ABI-L2-MCMIPF` has all 16 bands at 2 km in
  one ~300 MB file if that is simpler. Full disk = every 10 min. Fetch 14:00-20:30 UTC (40 files/band).
- Reading: netCDF4/xarray (`uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python netCDF4 h5netcdf`). The `CMI`
  variable is on the ABI fixed grid: projection `geos` with the file's own `goes_imager_projection`
  attributes (perspective_point_height, longitude_of_projection_origin, sweep_angle_axis='x').
  Reproject to equirectangular over lon -80..30, lat 25..80 with pyproj (`+proj=geos +h=... +lon_0=... +sweep=x`),
  ~4 km/px is plenty. Spain is at the disk edge for GOES-East (very oblique); Greenland/Iceland are fine.
- Normalise brightness honestly: divide reflectance by cos(solar zenith) so the daily cycle does not
  masquerade as the eclipse, THEN the eclipse is the anomaly. Show both raw and normalised if cheap.
