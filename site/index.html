How much ground per tile

A 256 px training tile is a window on the ground, and its width is a choice we make — not a property of the satellite. Every image below is a real 256 px tile of the same place, pulled today, differing only in how much world it holds.

footprint = 256 px × ground sample distance  →  MODIS z8 = 256 × 567 m = 145 km

Finer is not better. A feature is learnable only when it is coarser than a few pixels and smaller than the frame. Both ends bite.

Constraints

What the sources will and won't give us

SourceNative GSDDeepest zoomNarrowest 256 px windowCatch
MODIS Terra / Aqua250 mz973 kmDaily global, every day since 2000. Swath gaps cut black wedges through tiles.
VIIRS SNPP / NOAA-20375 mz973 kmSame ceiling, coarser sensor. Useful as a second look at the same day.
Sentinel-2 cloudless10 mz14+2.3 kmNo clouds, by construction — it is an annual cloud-free composite. One fixed epoch, no daily weather.
Landsat annual composite30 mz129 kmCloud-free annual mosaic, same limitation, coarser than Sentinel-2.

The two families do not overlap: anything that resolves terrain texture is a cloud-free composite, and anything that sees weather stops at 250 m. There is no source that gives us dune rhythm and today's sky.

Levers

Three ways to change the window

Change zoom

Move along the ladder

One step coarser doubles the footprint and doubles the GSD. Cheapest lever, and the only one that costs no extra compute — but it trades detail for extent one-for-one.

Stitch, then downscale

Same as a coarser zoom

Mosaicking 2×2 tiles and shrinking to 256 px gives exactly what one tile a zoom coarser gives. Worth doing only below the pyramid's own floor, or to dodge a swath gap.

Change output size

The only real gain

512 px at the same zoom keeps the GSD and doubles the footprint — the one way to hold detail and extent together. It costs roughly 3–4× the training time per kimg.

This refines an earlier, blunter claim of mine that the land set was simply unusable at z8. The Okavango tiles disprove that: continental-scale landforms read perfectly well at 148 km. What z8 cannot do is the texture scale — which is most of what the current region list asks for (dunes, mountains, farmland).
