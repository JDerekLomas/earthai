The lower 48

The whole continental United States from the geostationary satellite, every ten minutes, day and night, growing by a day each night for as long as the archive allows.

Seven by four map tiles from GOES-East, about 3.5 km per pixel, which is the same spacing as the HRRR weather model that covers exactly this ground. Marine layers on both coasts, afternoon convection over the Plains and the Gulf, fronts crossing the continent, and the night side with its city lights. The archive began on 10 August 2026 and a nightly fetch keeps adding to it.

Watch a day

The whole archive runs as one continuous clip, four hours a second, nothing cut at midnight. Or pick a day. Or hold the clock still: the same-hour flipbook shows one frame per day at a chosen local solar hour (solar time for the centre of the country, 98°W), so what moves is the weather, not the sun. GeoColor day clips run through daylight; infrared runs all 24 hours.

The month at a glance

Rows are days, columns are local solar hours at the centre of the country; the last column is 01:00, night. Read down a column for the day-to-day weather; read across a row for one day's convection building and collapsing. Empty cells are frames the archive does not have. Scroll inside the frame.
