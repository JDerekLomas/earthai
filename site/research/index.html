Painting clouds onto physics: what has been built

Nobody has published a model that takes a weather model's cloud fields in and paints a satellite frame out. Every part of it exists, and the recipe has been tested on the same imagery.

The decision this page serves: build a physics-conditioned generator (HRRR's cloud fields in, an image model painting kilometre-scale texture out, the NVIDIA CorrDiff pattern), or stay with latent-space controls in the GAN we have. Six survey agents fetched every paper and repository below; two of the load-bearing ones were re-read by hand. Items marked unverified could not be fetched (paywalls) and are reported from search snippets only.

The short version

  1. The architecture is settled and open. CorrDiff's recipe (a regression network predicts the mean field, a residual diffusion model paints the texture the regression smooths away) is Apache-licensed code in NVIDIA's PhysicsNeMo, and CorrDiff-Mini trains on a reduced HRRR-based set in about ten A100-hours, against roughly five thousand for the full CONUS model. On our L40S at €1.47 an hour that is a tens-of-euros experiment, not a project.
  2. The recipe has already won on GOES infrared. Chase et al. (2025) compared plain diffusion, latent diffusion, a CorrDiff-style residual model and a U-Net on 0–3 hour cloud nowcasts from geostationary infrared frames. The residual model was best, beat persistence, generated and decayed cloud rather than only moving it, and kept fine features longer. Its conditioning was earlier frames, not a physics model, but the target imagery is ours.
  3. Physics-fields-in, satellite-image-out has been done once, small. Hatanaka et al. (2023) trained a diffusion model to super-resolve coarse numerical weather prediction into weather-satellite-resolution cloud imagery for solar forecasting. The abstract states no pixel size; secondary reports say 64 to 128 px. No code found.
  4. No released weights do our job. The one downloadable CorrDiff checkpoint for the US takes 25 km GEFS in and produces HRRR-resolution wind, temperature and rain out: HRRR is its target, not its input, and nothing image-like comes out. StormCast (Apache 2.0, "research only") emulates HRRR itself and does emit composite radar reflectivity, the nearest thing to an image. Every satellite-image generator found is a single-channel research prototype without public weights.
  5. Our month is thin for this. Forty days is 960 hourly pairs, and the memory measurement on the month page says that is about seventy independent skies. Both archives go back years (HRRR to July 2014 on AWS; the native GOES record is on AWS too, which this pass did not fetch), and SEVIR pairs ten thousand GOES-16 storm events with radar at 5-minute cadence for the eastern US.
  6. The precondition is on the next page. None of this is worth building unless HRRR actually puts cloud where GOES saw it, hourly, at our scale, and beats both a shuffled-day floor and yesterday's frame. That is the agreement probe.

What it implies for building option 1

Physics fields in, a painted GOES frame out, for one place.

settled

Conditioning

HRRR on the frame grid is built (scripts/hrrr_grid.py): low, middle, high and total cloud fraction, the two simulated channels (10.7 µm window, 6.7 µm water vapour), cloud ceiling, plus terrain and solar hour. AI-CD says sea-surface temperature and inversion strength shape stratocumulus; HRRR carries both.

settled

Architecture

Regression mean plus residual diffusion. It is the open PhysicsNeMo code, it won on GOES infrared in Chase et al., and DiffCast shows the residual stage bolts onto any backbone. Target the infrared channel first (continuous through the night, one channel, a real temperature); GeoColor is a second head.

open

Data

960 hourly pairs, ~70 independent, is thin for diffusion. Both archives run for years: HRRR analyses back to 2014 are in the same bucket, and NOAA's native GOES-West record is on AWS (not fetched in this pass; GIBS only keeps a month). Or start on SEVIR's ten thousand paired GOES-16 events.

open

Cost

CorrDiff-Mini's reported ten A100-hours is the scale: tens of euros on the L40S per training run at 768 px or less. The full CONUS CorrDiff, ~5,000 A100-hours, is the number to never approach.

alternative

ControlNet route

GeoSynth's released ControlNet on Stable Diffusion 2.1 takes a raster in; swapping in an HRRR raster and GOES targets is a fine-tune, not a build. Nobody has shown it on a physical field, the prior is land photography, and the weights are 11 GB.

precondition

Truth

All of it only adds truth if HRRR knows where today's clouds are at our scale, and beats a shuffled day and yesterday's frame. Measured on the next page. If it fails, the physics fields are climatology with extra steps, and latent controls in the existing GAN are the honest option.

What could not be verified

  • Paywalled, reported from snippets only: Bikos et al. 2012 (synthetic imagery for model evaluation), NowcastNet's full text, Harris et al. 2023 (GAN emulation of km-scale NWP), DWD's MFASIS/LMSynSat, Yang et al. 2026 (3D cloud structure from geostationary imagery). Nothing on this page depends on them.
  • Contradicted the brief: MetNet-3 was listed as conditioning on NWP; Google's own blog says it does not. Removed as precedent.
  • Unanswered: the smallest training set and GPU budget for a ControlNet-style fine-tune on satellite imagery; neither relevant abstract discloses it. The paper bodies or repositories would.
  • Checked by hand, not only by agent: Hatanaka et al. 2023 and Chase et al. 2025 abstracts; the HRRR SBT channel assignment (decoded from a real file, not read from a table).
